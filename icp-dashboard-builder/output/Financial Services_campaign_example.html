How every asset was scored

Three axes, 1 to 5, against the ICP dossier. The gate is the lowest of the three: any axis under 4 and the asset is cut. Cut drafts stay on the page, struck through, with the reason.

W · Their words

  • 5 a verbatim phrase from a best customer
  • 4 close to their phrasing, or from an AE note (not verbatim)
  • 3 or less marketer-speak, or words from the anti-ICP

O · Outcome-led

  • 5 opens with the result they're buying
  • 4 opens with their pain or trigger, result follows
  • 3 or less opens with the product, a category or an adjective

V · Voice

  • 5 passes every rule in voice/voice-rules.md
  • 4 passes, but reads slightly like a brand
  • 3 or less hype word, em dash, banned phrase, or a claim with no proof

Flags on shipped lines: customer proof uses a customer result from the dossier (16 assets). confirm claim states a product capability the dossier can't prove (10 assets). Character counts are computed, not estimated. The build fails if a shipped line breaks a limit or a voice rule.

Exclusions, from the anti-ICP

Company exclusions

  • 10,001+ employees. Global in-house orchestrators run 40,000+ and route across tier-1 acquirers themselves. 4 of 4 lost.
  • Under 501 employees. Too small for the platform. The smallest best customer has 720.
  • Existing customers. All 40 won accounts: this is an acquisition campaign.
  • Hold list. ICP-fit accounts with no payments leader in seat.
  • Accounts with a live tender on lowest blended rate, or where the incumbent is invited to rebid. 0 of 14 tender deals became healthy customers.

Title exclusions

  • Procurement Manager, Category Manager - Finance & Payments. Decision maker in all 8 wrong-fit customers.
  • Finance Manager. Champion in all 4 too-small losses.
  • E-commerce Manager, Head of E-commerce. Not excluded outright, just not targeted: champion in 14 of 15 average customers.

Company size bands are a stand-in for payment volume, which LinkedIn can't filter on. The account list does the real work. The size bands only catch accounts that slip through.

Before launch

One page, one outcome. Every asset points to https://example.com/one-payment-stack: one payment stack for every market. The LinkedIn offers differ by role (FX savings model, business case template, technical breakdown, a call with the risk team). Each needs its own section on that page, or its own page, before the ads go live.
  • 10 product claims to confirm (flagged confirm claim): one integration for every market, single FX conversion, payout coverage in 55 countries, local methods where cards fail, one contract across markets, keeping the customer's fraud rules in year one, seller KYC done once, merging acquired stacks on one integration. The dossier shows customers wanted these. It doesn't prove the product does all of them.
  • 16 customer results (flagged customer proof): 61% to 78% LatAm approval rate, 3 days to same-day payouts, 4 days to same-day withdrawals, four PSPs down to one, FX savings paying for the switch in year one, sellers in 55 countries paid from one place. Real customers would need to approve each one, and there needs to be evidence behind it. None are named in the ads.
  • Known friction, don't overclaim: Asia coverage thinner than Europe (Pentland Travel), a second licence that took longer to onboard than promised (Esker Bet), a reconciliation file format that took work (Jarrow Holidays). No asset promises the opposite.
  • Small, in-sample ICP. The targeting rules come from 17 accounts and are measured on the same data. Treat the first quarter as a test of the list, not just the copy.
