Who stays and grows: the ICP from 60 won and lost accounts

Built from data/: 60 accounts (40 won, 20 lost), 162 contacts, 48 public signals, plus call notes, win/loss interviews and reviews. A best customer is a won account whose current ARR is at least 1.5x the ACV it signed. The cut sits in a natural gap: best customers run from 1.58x to 2.40x, the next group tops out at 1.24x.

Synthetic data. Every company, person, quote and number here is fictitious and was generated for this demo from written business assumptions, so the ICP can only confirm those assumptions. This page shows the method, not any real market.

The ICP

The Patchwork Multinational

A company with 40 to 140 physical sites spread across 12 to 35 countries, still running a legacy MPLS contract or a patchwork of regional carriers and local ISPs. A network leader at director level owns the mess personally and the CIO signs. Something has just changed the footprint or the contract clock: an acquisition in new countries, an announced country expansion or an MPLS renewal inside 12 months.

The name comes from a best customer's own description of their network: "a patchwork of local ISPs we picked up country by country" (Harwick Biosciences, discovery call).

The pattern: network owner champions, CIO signs, security and procurement evaluate. When procurement or finance signs, the account churns or stays flat. When security owns the budget, or nobody owns the network, the deal is lost, even at accounts with the right footprint.

Targeting rule (for the account list and routing)

1. Footprint (builds the list): countries_with_sites 12–35 AND site_count 40–140 AND current_network_setup IN (Legacy MPLS no SD-WAN, MPLS + local ISPs, Multiple regional carriers)

2. Buying group (qualifies it): a director-level network owner is the champion (Head of Network, Global Network Lead, Head of Network Infrastructure, Head of IT Infrastructure)

Segments and anti-ICP

Every account in the export lands in one group. The segments below the ICP are kept; the anti-ICP is excluded from targeting and routing.

Segment 2: One-bill consolidators

15 won accounts · ARR 1.04x to 1.24x · median ACV £149k · keep, lower priority
  • 7 to 15 countries, 17 to 59 sites. Includes the 3 accounts already running in-house SD-WAN.
  • Champion is manager-level IT Ops or IT Service (11 of 15). Finance decides in 7 of 15.
  • Came in through inbound content, outbound SDR, ABM or events; 5 of 11 known were at Awareness.
  • They buy one invoice, not a network change: "I want fewer invoices." "Finance wants one bill. That's the project."

Action: serve through content and nurture. No ABM spend, no SDR sequences built for them.

Segment 3: Right footprint, wrong buying group

6 lost accounts · £2.1m ACV lost · hold, re-engage on a change
  • Security-led (3): Brennan, Stellan, Radley. A new CISO and a board-level zero-trust programme came before pipeline in all 3; security blocked. "Security first, network second."
  • No network owner (3): Halden, Quarry, Bramwell. No network lead champion; at Halden and Quarry the Head of Network had left and was not backfilled. "I didn't have a network person to run the project, and I wasn't going to run it myself."
  • 5 of the 6 match the footprint rule (Radley has 11 countries). Footprint alone would target them.

Action: keep off active routing. Re-engage when a network leader is hired. Each sub-group is 3 accounts: an anecdote, not a pattern.

Anti-ICP: exclude from targeting and routing

Domestic price buyers

13 accounts on a single national carrier · 3 contracted, 5 churned, 5 lost · 0 retained at signed value
  • 1 to 4 countries, almost all sites in one country. Procurement or finance decides. No network-owner champion: at most an IT Manager.
  • Came in through a price-led tender or the pricing page, or small inbound (4 to 8 sites).
  • £531k signed at the 5 churned accounts, £0 left. The 3 contracted accounts fell from £218k to £134k.
  • "Price per store. The global part wasn't really relevant, we're almost all in Italy."

Exclude: current_network_setup = Single national carrier or countries_with_sites ≤ 4.

Incumbent re-bid tenders

12 accounts from RFP / tender · 6 lost, 5 churned, 1 contracted · 0 retained at signed value
  • Tender with price as the main criterion, incumbent invited to re-bid. Procurement decides.
  • Holds for multi-country tenders too (Calder 7 countries, Ostrava 8, Nesbury 6): all lost on price.
  • "The incumbent will match. They always do."

Exclude from routing: lead_source = RFP / tender where the incumbent re-bids and price per site is the award criterion.

Global fully managed WAN

4 lost accounts · £8.7m ACV lost · all to a tier-1 global carrier
  • 415 to 1,143 sites, 64 to 106 countries, 30,620 to 113,140 employees, on a global tier-1 MPLS.
  • They want one provider to run the whole WAN including LAN, Wi-Fi and hardware.
  • "You were honest that it's not what you do."
  • 4 accounts: an anecdote, but all 4 lost for the same stated reason.

Exclude: current_network_setup = Global MPLS from tier-1 carrier or site_count > 400.

Pains, desires and triggers

Pains

  • Too many carriers, and the network team doing procurement. Harwick, Kestrel, Whitmore.
  • New and acquired sites take months to connect. 3 to 4 months at Harwick; Kestrel's stores go live late because the line isn't ready (call notes); Moravia's acquired network a "black box" six months on.
  • Sites in countries the big carriers skip. Jorvik, Glenmoor.
  • Sites with no SLA holding up a bigger programme. Orwell's ERP go-live.
  • A renewal that rolls over by default. Juniper, Rosmer.

Desires

  • One contract, one portal, one number to call. Harwick, Moravia, Drummond.
  • Seeing every site in one place. Mereford; the Juniper CIO's "green or red" board view.
  • Security added later, without re-procuring. Juniper, Zeller, then delivered at Mereford and Drummond.
  • The internet layer done properly, not the whole WAN outsourced. Varne.
  • A price they can defend, not the cheapest. Moravia.
  • References from their own industry. Orwell asked for manufacturing; Moravia decided on a logistics reference call.

After the sale: expansion signals, not triggers

Signals dated after pipeline creation do not explain why an account bought. At best accounts they line up with growth: new countries opened (8 accounts: Moravia, Mereford, Ulvik, Rosmer, Drummond, Marlowe, Zeller, Whitmore) and SASE / zero-trust hiring (3: Ingram, Mereford, Zeller). Customer words match: SASE added in year two or three at Mereford and Drummond, a SASE review after 12 months agreed at Zeller. Recent ones to act on for expansion: Marlowe opened 5 countries (May 2026), Ingram hiring a SASE architect (May 2026), Whitmore opened 2 countries (Aug 2026).

Gaps and cross-checks

Left blank on purpose

  • No words on file for 3 best accounts: Ingram Manufacturing (ACC-0003), Hollin Logistics (ACC-0006), Marlowe Components (ACC-0050). They count in the numbers; nothing is said about their motivation.
  • Hollin Logistics has one contact (Global Network Lead, champion). No decision maker recorded and no intent stage.
  • Jorvik Freight: no pre-pipeline trigger in enrichment or calls.
  • Blocker at best accounts: none recorded, so none described.
  • Procurement's own reasons: only two thin mentions on file. Not enough to write this role's motivation.
  • 9 of 15 average accounts and 7 of 20 lost accounts have no call, interview or review.

Cross-checks against the calls

  • Matches: footprints quoted on calls and reviews match the CRM for Harwick (63 sites, 22 countries), Moravia (27), Glenmoor (31), Drummond (33), Whitmore (24), Jorvik (42 sites, 31 countries), Orwell (140, 21). Halden's call notes record the profile as a fit, consistent with it landing in the hold segment rather than the anti-ICP.
  • Mismatch, Kestrel Retail Group: Pablo Rossi says stores are in "about 40 countries"; the CRM says 35. Could be growth through franchise deals since the record was set. Check before using 35 in any list.
  • Timing, Moravia Logistics: Mia Wagner describes a September call after a March acquisition close, but the opportunity ran February to July 2021. The call date is not on file, so this cannot be reconciled from the data.
  • Triggers from calls, not enrichment: Juniper's MPLS renewal and Rosmer's renewal and SAP rollout only appear in calls and interviews. Enrichment under-reports renewals.

Limits

  • Synthetic and circular: the data was generated from assumptions, so this confirms them by design.
  • Small sample: 60 accounts. Global fully managed WAN (4), security-led (3) and no network owner (3) are anecdotes.
  • In-sample rule: the 17 of 18 targeting precision is fitted and measured on the same data.
